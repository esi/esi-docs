# Pantoscope

Pantoscope answers one question — *who am I looking at, and what does the record
already say about them* — from whatever the pilot happens to have on the clipboard.
There is one box. It works out what you pasted and reads accordingly.

## Features

**One consult box, six kinds of input.** A pilot's name, a whole local list, a
directional scan, a probe-scanner window, a system name, or a route written as
`Jita > Amarr`. The paste is classified and read without being told what it is.

**Pilot dossiers** from public record: employment history with alliance overlap,
killboard shape, solo proportion, where a pilot fights and who they fight beside.
Corporations and alliances have their own files.

**Local reads** that rank a roster rather than listing it, with timezone spread and
a continuity mode: paste local a second time and arrivals and departures are noted
against the previous read.

**Directional and probe scans** classified by hull class and by site, with wormhole
signatures appraised against live market prices.

**System files** with recent violence, neighbouring systems and jump traffic, and
**route advisories** that read a route before it is flown, segment by segment.

**A watchlist** of pilots, corporations, alliances and systems, held in the browser
rather than on a server, with export and import.

**Periscope**, a companion catalogue of every wormhole system in Anoikis with its
statics, phenomena and mass tolerances.

**Eight field circulars** — long-form written guides to exploration hacking,
Strategic Cruisers, stealth bombers, probe scanning, ghost sites and Sleeper Caches,
cloaked hunting, fullerite gas harvesting, and the naming of wormhole bookmarks. Six
of the eight carry an interactive desk that works the guide's rules for you.

## Notes

No account is required and none is offered. EVE SSO is optional, implemented
client-side with PKCE, and is used only to read the pilot's present location and to
set a destination; nothing is stored on a server. Data is drawn from ESI and from
zKillboard, and the site is explicit about what it cannot see — the limits are
documented on the About page rather than glossed over.
